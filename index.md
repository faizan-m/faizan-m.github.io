<div class="narrative-section">
    <p>
        I grew up doing contest math in Pakistan, where problem-solving meant finding structure in what looked like noise. That instinct carried forward — into robotics, where I built <a href="/work#ar-robot-visualization">interfaces that let people see how a robot reads a room</a>; into TensorFlow, where I <a href="/work#tensorflow">re-architected tf.function</a> around a type system that treats computation signatures the way a semiotician treats signs; and into Gemini and Search, where I now work on <a href="/work#search-ai">LLM-driven systems</a> that organize and reason over knowledge.
    </p>
    <p>
        The thread through all of it: how do you represent meaning in a way that machines can act on? Whether it is a robot expressing its internal state through augmented reality, a function's type signature encoding what it can compute, or a language model organizing search results into thematic structures — the question is always about the gap between symbol and understanding.
    </p>
    <p>
        I write occasionally on <a href="https://medium.com/@faizan.muham">Medium</a> about the places where these ideas meet philosophy, particularly the semiotics of Sufi thought and the boundaries of formal representation.
    </p>
</div>
